Lead Lifecycle.
We're replacing the old account-level lifecycle stages with a proper contact-level model. This page is the working spec for the CRM migration — new stage definitions, the Lead Status mapping, sources, and account taxonomy.
What's Changing

The old model had eight account-level lifecycle stages (Identified, Prospect, Pipeline, Customer, Churned, Partner, Competitor, Vendor) that were being used as a second lead status field — creating nonsensical mappings like "Meeting Set → Churned" and "Working → Customer." We're collapsing those to a single account stage of Lead and introducing a proper contact-level lifecycle model to track the buyer journey.

Handoff Protocol.
Who owns a lead at each transition, what must be documented before it moves, and how both sides confirm the handoff is complete. No verbal handoffs. Everything in the CRM.
Marketing → SDR (Sales Ready)

When a contact reaches the Sales Ready lifecycle stage, an SDR is assigned and the lead moves to Working status. The SDR SLA clock starts at assignment.

SDR → AE (Meeting Booked → Pipeline)

When a discovery call is booked, lead status moves to Meeting Set. The SDR completes the pre-meeting handoff doc before the AE joins the call. An opportunity is created after the call if the MEDDPIC entry criteria are met.

AE → CS (Closed Won)

CS handoff is initiated within 48 hours of contract signing. A handoff document is required — no verbal-only transitions. The AE introduces CS to the champion before stepping back from the relationship.

Handoff document.

AE completes the CS handoff doc: company context, key contacts, pain statement, what was sold, success criteria agreed during the deal, and any commitments made verbally or in writing.

Intro call.

AE, CS, and champion on a 30-minute intro call. AE introduces CS, recaps the pain and solution, confirms success criteria with the customer, then steps back. CS owns the relationship from that moment.

What good looks like.

Champion knows CS's name before the intro call. All MEDDPIC fields populated. Success criteria written and agreed by the customer. CS can run the account without calling the AE for context in week one.

What bad looks like.

Single Slack message from AE. CS discovers commitments that weren't in the contract. Champion gets a cold email from someone they've never heard of. RevOps reviews handoff quality quarterly.

Framework Reference
M
Metrics
Required at Stage 1
The quantified business impact. The prospect's number, not ours. Time saved, incidents resolved, MTTR reduced. Pain without a number is hard to justify internally.
"How many engineer-hours per week does your team spend on incident response? What would a 50% reduction mean for your roadmap?"
E
Economic Buyer
Required at Stage 3
The person who controls the budget. Not always the champion. Must be named and contacted. At NeuBird AI: typically VP Engineering or CTO.
"Who ultimately signs off on a new tool investment? Have you had a chance to loop them in yet?"
D
Decision Criteria
Required at Stage 4
How will they evaluate vendors? Technical fit, security posture, integration depth, pricing model, support. Know what they weight most and make sure we score on it.
"What would the perfect solution need to do for you to feel confident recommending it internally?"
D
Decision Process
Required at Stage 5
The steps from "we like it" to signed contract. Who else is involved? Legal? Security? IT? What's the procurement process? Map it early — process slippage is the #1 cause of close date misses.
"Once you decide to move forward, what does the actual approval process look like on your end?"
P
Proposal
Required at Stage 6
Formal proposal presented to the Economic Buyer. Pricing shared. Terms negotiated. All prior MEDDPIC elements must be complete before a proposal is presented.
"Does this proposal reflect what we discussed in terms of scope and pricing? Anything you'd need changed before taking it to your team?"
I
Identify Pain
Required at Stage 1
The specific, named operational pain. On-call fatigue, RCA taking 4+ hours, same incidents recurring. The champion must feel it personally — not abstractly. Pain without urgency doesn't close.
"Tell me about the last major incident. How long did it take to resolve? Who was involved at 2am?"
C
Champion
Required at Stage 2
The person selling for us when we're not in the room. Must have power, credibility, and access to the EB. Test them: "Can you get us 30 minutes with your VP?" If no, they're a coach, not a champion.
"Who else on your team would benefit from seeing this? Could you help us get them in the room?"

What we measure, how often, who owns each report, and what we do with the output. Reporting without action is data theater.
Reporting Cadence
Daily
SDR Activity Dashboard
  • MQLs created vs. SLA compliance (4-hr first touch rate)
  • Active sequences and reply rates by lead source
  • Inbound MQLs with no SDR activity — flagged at 9am
Weekly
Pipeline & Deal Review (Monday)
  • New pipeline created: source, stage, AE, lifecycle stage
  • Stage movement: deals advanced / stalled / slipped
  • MEDDPIC completeness: % of Stage 2+ deals with required fields
  • Forecast: AE commits vs. upside vs. pipeline coverage
  • Lead status distribution: MQL → Working → Meeting Set → Pipeline conversion
Monthly
Revenue & Funnel Report (First Monday)
  • Lifecycle stage conversion rates: Contact → Engaged → Sales Ready → Pipeline → Closed Won
  • Pipeline by source: events, inbound, outbound, partner, AI referral
  • Lead source performance: volume vs. conversion rate vs. deal size by source
  • MEDDPIC adoption: % of Stage 2+ opps with complete fields
  • Marketing attribution: first-touch and last-touch by channel and conversion point
Quarterly
QBR & Attribution Review
  • Pipeline coverage ratio vs. quota (target: 3× coverage)
